### [Order and Innovation — Rethinking What Innovation Really Is](https://rainerkattel.substack.com/p/order-and-innovation-rethinking-what) (Rainer Kattel, 27.11.2025)
> What if we’ve misunderstood innovation for most of the past century?
> 
> We’ve been trapped in a seductive metaphor: **creative destruction**.
> [...]
> What if [innovation] is **about order** — about how societies — companies, governments, non-governmental actors — repeatedly try to organise chaos into new forms of coherence, sense-making, and coordination?
> 
> W Brian Arthur describes technology not as a single invention moving inexorably forward, but as a continuous **[problem–solution–problem dynamic](https://sites.santafe.edu/~wbarthur/thenatureoftechnology.htm)**. Every technological solution generates new challenges: cars solve mobility but create pollution, congestion, and urban hollowing. AI solves problems of information but creates new problems of authority, trust, and exclusion.
> 
> Innovation, then, is fundamentally a **process of ordering** — of creating structures, practices, and shared understandings that help us live with, govern, and evolve new technologies.
> [...]
> This is where [Hans-Georg Gadamer’s notion of](https://web.mit.edu/kaclark/www/gadamer_truth_and_method.pdf) **[game or play](https://web.mit.edu/kaclark/www/gadamer_truth_and_method.pdf)** becomes powerful. In a game, everyone — players and spectators — operates within a shared framework of rules. But the rules are not entirely fixed and one-dimensional; they are interpreted, negotiated, and reshaped through the very act of playing. The game evolves because the players evolve.
> 
> Innovation works the same way. A company trying to make its technology a standard isn’t just outpacing competitors. It’s trying to **define the game** — the rules, the interpretations, the expectations. Innovation is an act of creating order _within_ complex technological and social systems.
